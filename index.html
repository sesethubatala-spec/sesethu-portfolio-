Research Interests & Professional Focus

My research and professional interests are shaped by questions about work, inequality, social change and the ways people navigate institutions and changing opportunities. My academic research and professional experiences have informed these interests, which continue to evolve across social research, organisational contexts and educational content development.

Youth Employability and Transitions into Work

My research examines how young people understand work and navigate the transition from education into employment. I am interested in the relationship between graduates' expectations, their experiences of entering the labour market, and the structural conditions that shape their opportunities.

Labour Markets and Changing Expectations of Work

My work in industrial sociology and labour studies has informed my interest in how labour markets, employment structures and changing social expectations shape people's relationship with work. I am particularly interested in the relationship between workers' aspirations, the organisation of work, and the broader dynamics between labour and capital.

Precarious Employment and Employment Insecurity

I am interested in how insecure, informal and non-standard employment arrangements shape workers' experiences, opportunities and agency. This includes the ways employment insecurity affects workers' wellbeing, their ability to exercise choice, and their prospects for sustainable livelihoods.

Labour Rights, Inequality, and Social Structures

My academic work has explored the relationship between formal labour protections and workers' knowledge of their rights. I am interested in how social inequalities and institutional structures influence access to rights, particularly among domestic workers and people in low-wage or informal employment.

Qualitative Research and Research Communication

My research experience includes interviewing, qualitative data collection and thematic analysis. I am interested in how research can capture people's experiences with rigour and care, and how findings can be communicated clearly and meaningfully to academic, professional and wider audiences.

Educational Content Development and Learning Design

My content development experience has strengthened my interest in how complex ideas can be translated into accessible, structured learning materials. I am particularly interested in connecting critical thinking and real-world issues with engaging educational content that responds to different learner needs and contexts.

Research

Graduate Transitions and the Changing World of Work

My master's research examines how recent graduates conceptualise work and what this reveals about South Africa's labour market. The study explores how graduate expectations interact with fragmented employment structures, insecure work arrangements, and broader conditions in a constrained labour market. It draws on qualitative approaches to understand the subjective dimensions of the school-to-work transition in a context of graduate unemployment and underemployment.

Capabilities demonstrated

  • Qualitative research design
  • Labour-market analysis
  • Academic writing and argumentation
  • Conceptual and theoretical engagement
Research

Labour Rights and Inequality

My honours research investigated the extent to which domestic workers in South Africa are aware of their rights under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act of 1997. The study examined the gap between formal legal protections and workers' practical knowledge of those rights, situating this gap within the unequal employment relationships and broader social structures that shape domestic workers' experiences and limit their bargaining power.

Capabilities demonstrated

  • Research into labour law and workers' rights
  • Analysis of unequal employment relationships
  • Qualitative fieldwork and data analysis
